## Executive Summary

Insignia Financial Ltd, Australia's largest listed diversified wealth
management group with roughly $340 billion in funds under management and
administration (FUMA), was acquired by CC Capital Partners in a scheme of
arrangement that completed in April 2026, ending a 16-month competitive
process that at various points involved three private equity bidders: CC
Capital, Bain Capital and Brookfield Asset Management.

The final scheme price of $4.80 per share implied an equity value of
approximately $3.3 billion and a 57% premium to the undisturbed share price
of $3.06 on 11 December 2024. Notably, the final price came in below the
~$5.00 per share peak reached during the competitive phase of the process in
March 2025, after both Bain Capital and Brookfield withdrew. This case study
reconstructs the process, values each stage of the bidding using a
consistent EV/EBITDA framework, and sets out the judgment calls a Financial
Sponsors or M&A advisory team would have had to make at each stage.

## 1. Company Overview

Insignia Financial (formerly IOOF, and prior to that incorporating the MLC
business acquired from National Australia Bank in 2021) operated across four
lines of business: Master Trust (superannuation), Wrap platforms, Advice
(including the Shadforth network), and Asset Management (including
multi-asset and fixed income capabilities). The FY25 result marked a turning
point operationally: statutory NPAT returned to positive territory at $16.1
million after a $185.3 million loss in FY24, driven by the completion of the
MLC-NAB separation, resolution of legacy advice remediation costs, and
delivery of cost optimisation targets.

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | TTM (Dec-25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($m) | 1,948 | 1,942 | 1,581 | 1,642 |
| EBITDA ($m) | 289.7 | 130.1 | 393.3 | 412.7 |
| EBITDA Margin | 14.9% | 6.7% | 24.9% | 25.1% |
| Statutory NPAT ($m) | 51.4 | (185.3) | 16.1 | 111.7 |
| FUMA ($bn, period-end/avg) | n/a | 319.6 | 323.0 (avg) | 342.0 |

The FY24 EBITDA trough reflects roughly $237 million of unusual items,
principally advice remediation, legal settlements and transformation costs
tied to the NAB separation, which is why FY25's clean result was central to
how bidders underwrote forward earnings.

## 2. Deal Timeline and the Shape of the Contest

The process ran through three distinct phases: an initial approach and
price discovery period, a competitive three-way phase with exclusivity
granted to two bidders simultaneously, and a final consolidation phase once
two of the three bidders withdrew.

**Phase 1 — Initial Approach (Dec 2024 – Jan 2025)**

- Bain Capital made an initial non-binding indicative proposal in December
  2024, using the 11 December 2024 closing price of $3.06 as the last
  undisturbed reference point.
- CC Capital and Bain Capital both revised proposals to $4.60 per share by
  late January 2025.

**Phase 2 — Three-Way Competitive Process (Feb – May 2025)**

- Brookfield Capital Partners (UK) entered the process in February 2025,
  matching the $4.60 offer and securing limited due diligence access —
  turning a two-horse race into a genuine three-way contest.
- By March 2025, Bain Capital and CC Capital had both sweetened their bids
  to $5.00 per share, and Insignia's board signed exclusivity deeds with
  both simultaneously, an unusual structure that kept optionality alive
  while narrowing the field.
- The exclusivity period was extended to allow both remaining bidders to
  arrange debt financing and complete due diligence.

**Phase 3 — Consolidation and Binding Offer (May 2025 – Apr 2026)**

- Bain Capital withdrew in May 2025, citing macro uncertainty in global
  markets as the reason it could not proceed to a binding proposal — a
  reminder that even at exclusivity stage, financing and macro conditions
  can still kill a deal.
- Brookfield did not match the improved terms and dropped out of
  contention around the same time, leaving CC Capital as the sole remaining
  bidder.
- Insignia entered a binding Scheme Implementation Deed with CC Capital
  (via Daintree BidCo) on 22 July 2025, at $4.80 per share — below the
  $5.00 peak reached during active competition.
- An independent expert deemed the offer fair and reasonable in February
  2026; shareholders voted to approve in April 2026 with support in the
  high-80s to high-90s percentage range; the Federal Court approved the
  scheme on 16 April 2026; and the transaction completed on 28 April 2026,
  with Insignia delisted from the ASX on 29 April 2026.

## 3. Valuation Across the Bidding Process

Applying a consistent EV/EBITDA framework across each stage of the process
(full detail and formulas in the accompanying Excel model) shows how the
implied multiple moved as the contest evolved:

| Stage | Price/Share | Equity Value | EV | EV/FY25 EBITDA | Premium to Undisturbed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undisturbed (11-Dec-24) | $3.06 | $2,044m | $2,477m | 6.3x | - |
| Initial matched bids (Jan 2025) | $4.60 | $3,073m | $3,505m | 8.9x | 50.3% |
| Peak competitive bid (Mar 2025) | $5.00 | $3,340m | $3,773m | 9.6x | 63.4% |
| Final Scheme price (Jul 2025) | $4.80 | $3,206m | $3,639m | 9.3x | 56.9% |

The 56.9% premium implied by this framework closely matches Insignia's own
disclosed 57% premium figure, which is a useful sense-check on the net debt
and share count assumptions used (set out in the model's Valuation Analysis
tab). The more interesting story is the ~4% price decline from the $5.00
peak to the $4.80 final price: once the field narrowed from three bidders
to one, CC Capital had materially less competitive tension to price
against, and the final terms reflect that shift in bargaining power as much
as any change in Insignia's underlying fundamentals.

## 4. Key Judgment Calls (Advisory Perspective)

If advising Insignia's board through this process, several decision points
stand out as genuinely difficult calls rather than obvious ones:

- Running dual exclusivity in March 2025 (signing exclusivity deeds with
  both Bain and CC Capital simultaneously) kept competitive tension alive
  for longer than a single-bidder exclusivity period would have, which
  likely contributed to the $4.60 → $5.00 uplift. This is a structure worth
  understanding in detail, since it's an unusual sequencing choice most
  retail-facing M&A commentary glosses over.
- Granting Brookfield due diligence access in February 2025, after two
  bidders had already emerged, was a deliberate choice to maximise
  competitive tension rather than move quickly to a binding deal with the
  two most advanced parties — a trade-off between speed/certainty and price
  maximisation.
- Once Bain withdrew citing macro uncertainty, the board's decision to
  proceed with CC Capital at $4.80 rather than push harder or accept
  execution risk by waiting for market conditions to improve reflects a
  preference for certainty once competitive tension had genuinely broken
  down — a defensible but not risk-free call, given the independent expert
  still had to separately confirm fairness.
- The MAC (material adverse change) clause tied to a forward-looking
  reference EBITDA, rather than a historic figure, as confirmed by CFO
  David Chalmers on the FY25 earnings call, meant Insignia's near-term
  earnings trajectory mattered directly to deal certainty, not just to the
  headline valuation.

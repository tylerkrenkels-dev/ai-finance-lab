## Executive Summary

Perpetual Limited, one of Australia's oldest financial services groups
(Asset Management, Corporate Trust, and until recently Wealth Management),
has been the subject of three successive non-binding takeover proposals
from EQT AB since 1 July 2026, rising from $21.64 to $22.07 to $22.50 per
share. The Perpetual board has rejected all three, most recently on 28-29
July 2026, but has now granted EQT limited, non-exclusive access to
non-public information to help formulate an improved offer. As of the most
recent traded price, the stock sits meaningfully below the latest rejected
bid, suggesting the market is not yet pricing in a high probability of a
higher offer or deal completion.

The situation is complicated by a second, related transaction: Perpetual
has separately agreed to sell its Wealth Management division to Bain
Capital for $500 million upfront plus up to $100 million in contingent
earnout, targeted to complete in Q4 CY2026. Completion of that sale is
itself a stated condition of any binding EQT transaction, meaning two deals
now need to land in sequence before shareholders see a resolution. This is
not Perpetual's first encounter with private equity: a Scheme
Implementation Deed with KKR, covering Wealth Management and Corporate
Trust, was terminated in February 2025 after Perpetual had already begun
operationally separating its divisions in preparation for it.

## 1. Company Overview

Perpetual (ASX: PPT), founded in 1886, operates two remaining core
divisions following the Wealth Management divestment: Asset Management, a
global multi-boutique manager spanning equities, credit, fixed income and
multi-asset strategies (including boutiques such as J O Hambro, Barrow
Hanley and TSW) with $219.2 billion in AUM as at 31 December 2025; and
Corporate Trust, a fiduciary and digital services business spanning Debt
Market Services, Managed Funds Services, and Digital and Markets,
administering roughly $1.3 trillion in funds under administration.

FY25's large statutory loss reflects a $134.6 million impairment,
predominantly relating to the J O Hambro boutique, alongside costs tied to
the (ultimately terminated) KKR transaction and the ongoing Simplification
Program. UPAT — Perpetual's preferred underlying measure — has been
comparatively stable, down only 1% in FY25 and up 12% in 1H26, driven by
cost discipline more than revenue growth. Asset Management continues to see
net outflows even as AUM rises on market performance, a dynamic worth
understanding for any valuation view: flows, not just markets, drive the
medium-term revenue base a buyer is underwriting.

## 2. The Bidding Situation

**Background — the KKR Scheme (2024-25)**

- Through 2024, Perpetual operationally separated its three divisions in
  preparation for a Scheme of Arrangement under which KKR would acquire
  Wealth Management and Corporate Trust, leaving Asset Management as a
  standalone listed entity.
- That Scheme Implementation Deed was terminated in February 2025.
  Perpetual has not disclosed the specific reason in the sources reviewed
  for this case study, but continued the separation work regardless, since
  it now supports the current, differently-structured operating model.

**Current Situation — EQT AB (July 2026 onward)**

- 1 July 2026: Perpetual entered a trading halt following market reports of
  an assembled EQT bid; shares surged on the news. EQT's initial proposal,
  via an entity called Windflower Pte Limited, came in at $21.64 per share
  — a premium of almost 40% to the undisturbed price — and was rejected the
  same day as highly conditional and inadequate.
- 15/17 July 2026: EQT revised to $22.07 per share, a 2% increase;
  rejected.
- 26 July 2026: EQT revised further to $22.50 per share, another 2%
  increase (4% above the original proposal); the board again concluded
  this was not in shareholders' best interests, but for the first time
  agreed to grant EQT limited, non-exclusive, confidential access to
  non-public information to help formulate an improved offer.
- Completion of the separate Bain Capital Wealth Management sale is a
  disclosed condition of any binding EQT transaction — meaning EQT's
  economics are effectively underwriting the post-divestment Asset
  Management and Corporate Trust stub, not the group as it stands today.

**Parallel Transaction — Bain Capital / Wealth Management**

- Perpetual has agreed to sell its Wealth Management division to Bain
  Capital Private Equity for $500 million upfront cash plus up to $100
  million in contingent performance-based earnout, targeting completion in
  Q4 CY2026 subject to regulatory and court approvals.
- Management has guided to a pro-forma net debt/EBITDA of approximately
  0.2x following completion, reflecting both the sale proceeds and
  continued gross debt paydown under the Simplification Program.

The 39.6% premium implied by the initial $21.64 proposal matches press
reporting of "almost 40%" closely, a useful sense-check on the assumptions
used. The more revealing figure is the gap between the latest rejected bid
($22.50) and the most recent traded price ($19.50): a $3.00, or roughly
13%, discount. In an unresolved bid situation, this gap is effectively the
market's own probability-weighted estimate of deal completion risk — a
meaningful discount signals the market sees real chance of the process
stalling, a lower final price, or a long, uncertain path (consistent with
EQT's access being limited and non-exclusive rather than a full
exclusivity arrangement).

## 4. Key Judgment Calls (Advisory Perspective)

Several elements of this situation are genuinely live judgment calls rather
than settled facts, which is exactly what makes it a useful one to track in
real time:

- Sequencing two linked transactions (Wealth Management sale to Bain, then
  a potential EQT deal for the remainder) creates real completion risk and
  negotiating complexity — EQT is effectively conditioning its offer on a
  transaction Perpetual doesn't fully control the timing of, since it also
  needs regulatory and court approval.
- Granting limited, non-exclusive due diligence access after three
  rejections, rather than either accepting a bid or shutting the process
  down, is a deliberate middle path: it keeps optionality open without
  granting EQT the negotiating leverage that full exclusivity would confer.
- The board's repeated rejections despite each bid being a premium to the
  undisturbed price reflect a view that intrinsic value (post-Wealth
  Management sale, at reduced leverage, potentially benefiting from
  re-rating as a cleaner Asset Management/Corporate Trust story) exceeds
  what's currently on the table — a bet worth stress-testing against the
  AUM outflow trend in Asset Management.
- The upcoming FY26 results (27 August 2026) will be a genuine inflection
  point for this analysis — updated guidance, confirmed leverage
  post-Wealth Management sale, and any commentary on the EQT process will
  materially move the picture and should prompt an update to this model.

## 5. What This Demonstrates

Unlike a completed transaction, this case study is designed to be tracked
and updated in real time — refreshing the valuation table as new proposals
or rejections are announced, and revisiting the leverage math once the
Wealth Management sale completes. That live-tracking discipline, reading
each ASX announcement, updating the model same-day, and forming a fresh
view on what the market's reaction implies, is closer to what a Financial
Sponsors or M&A analyst actually does day to day than a backward-looking
case study alone can demonstrate.

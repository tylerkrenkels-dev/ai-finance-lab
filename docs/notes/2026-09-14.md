# US Rates Rise Sharply While Equities Retreat; Commodity Weakness Persists

US Treasury yields climbed notably over the past month, with the 2-year yield reaching 4.63% and the 10-year at 4.96%, while the Federal Reserve held rates steady at 3.63%. The US 2s10s curve remains modest at 33.0 basis points. Australian yields have also drifted higher, with the 3-year at 4.82% and 10-year at 5.2%, though data through September 9 is now stale. The ASX 200 declined 0.89% on the day and 5.51% over the month. Gold and copper both retreated, while oil prices showed strength earlier in the week. AUD/USD carry dynamics have been volatile, with the 1-week window showing negative annualized returns of -24.6% despite a stable 0.72% rate differential.

- US 2-year Treasury yield rose 7.0 basis points to 4.63% on the day and 41.0 basis points over one month, outpacing the 10-year's more modest 1.0 basis point daily move.
- The US 2s10s curve slope stands at 33.0 basis points, reflecting a relatively flat yield curve structure.
- Australian 3-year and 10-year government bond yields reached 4.82% and 5.2% respectively as of September 9, with the AU 3s10s slope at 38.2 basis points; Australian data is now stale.
- The AU-US 10-year spread widened to 24.2 basis points, indicating a modest premium for Australian duration.
- The RBA Cash Rate Target remained anchored at 4.35%, unchanged over all measured periods.
- Gold declined 0.91% on the day to 4335.1001 USD/oz, with losses of 2.66% over one week and 1.03% over one month.
- Copper fell 1.83% to 6.3985 USD/lb on the day, down 4.8% over the week and 3.05% over the month.
- Brent Crude and WTI Crude both posted gains earlier in the week but data is stale as of September 9; Brent stood at 109.51 USD/bbl and WTI at 97.26 USD/bbl.
- The ASX 200 declined 0.89% on the day to 8741.2002, with broader weakness of 2.94% over one week and 5.51% over one month; US CPI data is stale as of August 1.

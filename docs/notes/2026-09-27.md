# US Yields Rise Sharply While Gold Retreats; Australian Rates Steady Amid Mixed Commodity Signals

US Treasury yields advanced across the curve this week, with the 2-year climbing 20.0 basis points and the 10-year rising 24.0 basis points, keeping the 2s10s slope at a modest 31.0 basis points. The effective federal funds rate held steady at 3.88% over the past week after a 25.0 basis point move over the month. Australian rates showed divergent momentum: the RBA cash rate target remained anchored at 4.35%, while the 3-year yield declined 5.6 basis points weekly to 4.93%, and the 10-year fell 10.3 basis points to 5.25%, leaving the AU 3s10s slope at 31.1 basis points. The AU-US 10-year spread stands at 6.5 basis points. Gold has weakened substantially, falling 7.96% over the month to 4292.8999 USD/oz, while copper has edged higher by 2.22% to 6.7335 USD/lb. Energy markets remain under pressure, with Brent crude at 114.89 USD/bbl and WTI at 96.41 USD/bbl, though data for these series is dated. The ASX 200 declined 5.45% over the month to 8665.0. Note: US CPI data is stale as of 2026-08-01; AUD/USD, crude oil, and natural gas quotes are dated 2026-09-22 or earlier; and Australian commodity prices are from 2026-07-01.

- US 2-year Treasury yield at 4.87%, up 20.0 basis points over the week and 63.0 basis points over the month.

# US Rates Rise Across the Curve as RBA Holds; Commodity Weakness Persists

US Treasury yields advanced across the curve this week, with the 2-year climbing 5.0 basis points to 4.83% and the 10-year rising 4.0 basis points to 5.28%, while the Federal Funds Rate remained steady at 3.88%. The RBA held its cash rate target at 4.6% following a 25 basis point increase last week. Australian bond yields have retreated from recent highs, with the 3-year at 4.92% and 10-year at 5.34%, though Australian data is stale as of September 30. The US 2s10s curve slope stands at 45.0 basis points, while the AU 3s10s slope is 42.2 basis points. Commodity markets showed mixed signals: gold recovered modestly to 4179.2002 USD/oz, copper gained 1.49% to 6.679 USD/lb, but crude oil prices declined sharply, with Brent falling 5.01% to 113.96 USD/bbl. The ASX 200 edged higher, gaining 0.59% to 8733.0996, though it remains down 3.03% over the month. AUD/USD carry trades have turned negative on a weekly and monthly basis despite a 0.72% rate differential.

- US 2-year Treasury yield at 4.83%, up 5.0 basis points daily and 44.0 basis points over the month; 10-year at 5.28%, up 4.0 basis points daily and 49.0 basis points monthly.
- Federal Funds Rate unchanged at 3.88% over the past week, though up 25.0 basis points from one month ago.
- RBA cash rate target held at 4.6% following a 25.0 basis point increase one week prior.
- US 2s10s curve slope at 45.0 basis points; AU 3s10s slope at 42.2 basis points; AU-US 10Y spread at 6.4 basis points.
- Gold at 4179.2002 USD/oz, up 0.14% daily and 0.26% weekly, but down 6.64% over the month.

- Brent crude at 113.96 USD/bbl, down 5.01% daily (data as of September 29); WTI at 96.16 USD/bbl, down 3.23% daily.

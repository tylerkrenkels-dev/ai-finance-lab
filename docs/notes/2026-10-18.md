# US Yields Retreat While Australian Rates Hold Steady; Commodity Strength Persists

US Treasury yields declined sharply over the past day, with the 10-year falling 9.0 basis points to 5.1% and the 2-year dropping 5.0 basis points to 4.77%, steepening the 2s10s curve to 33.0 basis points. The Federal Funds Rate remained anchored at 3.88%, unchanged over the week and month. In Australia, the RBA Cash Rate held at 4.6% while the 3-year and 10-year government bond yields edged higher on the day, with the 3s10s curve at 40.5 basis points. The AU-US 10-year spread stands at 21.4 basis points. Energy markets rallied, with WTI crude up 7.25% over the week to 101.91 USD/bbl and Brent crude at 126.55 USD/bbl. Gold recovered to 4195.7002 USD/oz after recent weakness, while copper edged up 0.61% to 6.571 USD/lb. The ASX 200 gained 1.0% on the day to 8806.7002. Note that US CPI data is stale as of 2026-09-01, AUD/USD pricing is stale as of 2026-10-09, and commodity data for iron ore and coal are stale as of 2026-07-01.

- US 10-year Treasury yield fell 9.0 basis points to 5.1% on the day, while the 2-year declined 5.0 basis points to 4.77%, with the 2s10s slope at 33.0 basis points.

- AU-US 10-year spread stands at 21.4 basis points, reflecting the yield differential between the two markets.
- WTI crude surged 7.25% over the week to 101.91 USD/bbl, while Brent crude advanced to 126.55 USD/bbl, up 4.37% over the month.
- Gold recovered to 4195.7002 USD/oz, gaining 0.35% on the day after declining 5.18% over the past month.

- ASX 200 index rose 1.0% on the day to 8806.7002, with gains of 1.03% over the week and 1.27% over the month.

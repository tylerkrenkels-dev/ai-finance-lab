# Treasury Yields Decline Sharply Across the Curve as Rate Expectations Ease

US Treasury yields fell notably over the past week, with the 2-year yield at 4.65% and the 10-year at 4.96%, both down from a week prior. The 2s10s curve slope remains modest at 31.0 basis points. The Federal Funds rate held steady at 3.88%, while the RBA Cash Rate Target remained anchored at 4.6% following a 25 basis point increase last month. Australian yields moved higher on the day, with the 3-year at 4.91% and 10-year at 5.28%. Gold retreated from recent highs to 4012.8 USD/oz, while copper edged higher to 6.5035 USD/lb. The ASX 200 was essentially flat at 8795.2002. Several commodity and FX data points are stale, and US CPI data has not been updated since September.

- US 2-year Treasury yield fell 16.0 basis points to 4.65% over the past day and 12.0 basis points over the week, while the 10-year declined 17.0 basis points daily and 14.0 basis points weekly to 4.96%.
- The US 2s10s curve slope stands at 31.0 basis points, indicating a modest steepening bias relative to recent extremes.
- The Federal Funds rate remained unchanged at 3.88% across all measured periods.
- The RBA Cash Rate Target held at 4.6%, having risen 25.0 basis points over the past month.
- Australian 3-year and 10-year yields rose on the day, with the 3-year at 4.91% and 10-year at 5.28%, while the AU 3s10s slope measured 36.9 basis points.
- The AU-US 10-year spread widened to 31.7 basis points, reflecting relative yield dynamics between the two markets.
- Gold declined to 4012.8 USD/oz, down 4.36% over the week and 7.14% over the month.
- Copper at 6.5035 USD/lb gained 0.42% on the day but remains down 1.03% weekly and 2.87% monthly.
- The ASX 200 closed essentially flat at 8795.2002, with minimal daily movement but modest monthly gains of 0.34%.

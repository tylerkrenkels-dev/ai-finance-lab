# US Yields Retreat Across the Curve as Fed Holds Steady; Australian Rates Rise on RBA Tightening

US Treasury yields declined across the curve this week, with the 2-year falling 9.0 basis points and the 10-year dropping 15.0 basis points, while the Federal Funds Rate remained anchored at 3.88%. The US 2s10s curve slope stands at 32.0 basis points. In contrast, the RBA raised its Cash Rate Target by 25.0 basis points to 4.85% over the past month, supporting Australian yields. The AU-US 10Y spread widened to 30.8 basis points. Commodity markets showed mixed signals: oil prices retreated sharply over the month, gold rallied, and copper declined. The ASX 200 advanced 0.54% on the day. Several data series carry staleness warnings, including US CPI, Australian bond yields, AUD/USD, and commodity prices for iron ore and coal.

- US 2s10s curve slope at 32.0 basis points; AU 3s10s slope at 29.2 basis points; AU-US 10Y spread at 30.8 basis points.
- Brent Crude Oil declined 21.02% over one month to 107.03 USD/bbl; WTI Crude at 99.64 USD/bbl, up 1.79% monthly despite recent weakness.
- Gold rallied 1.75% over one week to 4102.0 USD/oz, though down 1.32% over the month; Copper fell 5.16% monthly to 6.2455 USD/lb.
- Henry Hub Natural Gas at 2.63 USD/MMBtu, down 9.31% over one month; ASX 200 at 9007.5, up 3.11% over one month and 0.54% on the day.

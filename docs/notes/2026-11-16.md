# US Rates Rise Across the Curve as Australian Assets Face Headwinds

US Treasury yields advanced sharply over the past week, with the 2-year climbing 40.0 basis points and the 10-year rising 26.0 basis points, keeping the 2s10s curve at a modest 24.0 basis point slope. The Federal Funds Rate held steady at 3.88%, while the RBA maintained its cash rate target at 4.85% following a 25.0 basis point increase last month. Australian government bond yields have retreated over the month despite recent daily gains, with the 3-year at 4.73% and 10-year at 5.04%, producing a 31.1 basis point 3s10s slope. The Australian dollar weakened to 0.6875 USD per AUD, with carry returns deteriorating sharply on a one-day basis. Gold retreated 6.38% over the month to 3904.1001 USD/oz, while copper declined 6.53% to 6.086 USD/lb. The ASX 200 edged down 0.28% to 9057.2002 on the day but remains up 2.84% over the month.

- US 2-year Treasury yield at 4.86% after a 40.0 basis point weekly gain; 10-year at 5.1% up 26.0 basis points over the same period.
- US 2s10s curve slope stands at 24.0 basis points, maintaining a modest positive slope.
- RBA cash rate target unchanged at 4.85% following a 25.0 basis point hike in October.
- Australian 3-year and 10-year yields at 4.73% and 5.04% respectively, with AU-US 10Y spread at -5.6 basis points.
- AUD/USD at 0.6875, down 2.32% over the week; one-day annualised carry return deteriorated to -449.97% as the currency weakened.
- Gold declined 6.38% over the month to 3904.1001 USD/oz, with a 0.46% daily gain.
- Copper fell 6.53% over the month to 6.086 USD/lb, showing relative stability on the day at 0.11% change.
- Brent Crude at 92.14 USD/bbl, up 2.2% on the day but down 29.14% over the month; WTI at 89.62 USD/bbl.
